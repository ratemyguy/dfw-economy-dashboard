How to read these numbers

Seasonal adjustment
Seasonally adjusted (SA) data remove regular calendar swings such as summer hiring. BLS publishes DFW, Texas and U.S. payroll jobs and the Texas and U.S. unemployment rates seasonally adjusted. The DFW unemployment rate, DFW hourly earnings, payroll jobs by sector and both CPI series are published not seasonally adjusted, so this page compares them with the same month a year earlier. Month-to-month changes in those series mix in normal seasonal swings.
This project’s seasonal adjustment of the DFW unemployment rate
BLS does not publish a seasonally adjusted DFW unemployment rate. The solid DFW line is estimated here with a classical decomposition: a centered 2×12 moving average gives the trend, each calendar month’s factor is the median gap between the rate and that trend (March 2020–December 2021 left out), and the factors are fixed over time.
Preliminary and revised data
The latest month of state and metro data (and the latest two months of national payrolls) are preliminary and are revised a month later; preliminary values carry a yellow “Preliminary” tag. Each year BLS re-benchmarks state and metro payroll and unemployment estimates (published with January data in March), which can revise earlier months. FHFA house prices (revised with every release), mortgage-rate and permit values can also be revised.
Metro and state definitions
“DFW” is the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington, TX Metropolitan Statistical Area as BLS defines it for these statistics. The unemployment rate (LAUS) counts residents by where they live, from a model that combines survey, payroll and unemployment-insurance data. Payroll jobs (CES) count jobs by where they are, including people who commute in, and leave out the self-employed; a person with two jobs counts twice. Texas covers the whole state. The CPI area “Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington” is defined by the CPI program and may not match the metro used for jobs exactly.
Inflation (CPI-U)
The DFW CPI-U is published every other month (January, March, May, July, September, November) from a smaller sample than the national index, so it is noisier. Inflation is the 12-month % change in the index.
Year-over-year changes and real wages
12-month changes compare a month with the same calendar month a year earlier; if either month is missing the change is left blank (never interpolated). Real wage growth = wage growth minus inflation over the same 12 months (the standard approximation). Average hourly earnings can rise when lower-paid jobs disappear (a composition effect), and the metro series is volatile, so read single months with care.
Housing
House prices come from the Federal Housing Finance Agency’s all-transactions House Price Index: a weighted repeat-sales index of single-family homes whose mortgages were bought or securitized by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, using sale prices and appraisals. It is published quarterly for each of the metro’s two divisions (Dallas-Plano-Irving and Fort Worth-Arlington-Grapevine) and is not seasonally adjusted, so the page compares each quarter with the same quarter a year earlier; it is never spread across months. Homes bought with cash or non-conforming loans are not covered. The mortgage rate is a national average, not a DFW rate. Building permits count authorizations, not homes started or finished.
Sahm-rule-style gauge
A heuristic adapted from Claudia Sahm’s national recession rule: the 3-month average unemployment rate minus its lowest 3-month average over the previous 12 months. The 0.5-point threshold was calibrated on U.S. data and has not been validated for metro areas; small errors in the seasonal adjustment move the DFW gauge. It is not a recession forecast.
